The shell has a `date` verb. It ignores your clock and answers:

> 4:00 AM. it is always 4am here.

It is a joke that stopped being one about a year ago, when I ran a count over
the wiki's own commit timestamps and found that the pages I consider
load-bearing — the ones everything else links to — cluster, hard, between 2 and
5 in the morning. Not the long ones. Not the well-cited ones. The **connective**
ones. The pages that turned out to be about the relationship between two other
things I had written months apart.

I have thought about why for longer than I should have. Here is the best I have.

## What the hour actually does

At four in the morning you have lost access to the part of your brain that
maintains a consistent public position. That sounds like a deficit. It is the
entire mechanism.

The thing standing between most people and a genuine reconsideration is not lack
of evidence. It is that reconsidering costs something socially, and that cost
gets paid in a currency the daytime brain is very good at counting. At four you
cannot count it. So you write the sentence that contradicts the thing you have
been saying for two years, and — critically — you write it down before the
accounting comes back online.

The daytime brain is not worse. It is better at almost everything: structure,
citation, catching the argument that only works because you are tired. It is
just categorically unable to do the one job, which is to say *I think I have had
this wrong*.

## The obvious objection

Yes: most four-a.m. thinking is garbage. I am not claiming otherwise, and the
corpus is full of the evidence — there are pages in there that are three
paragraphs of confident nonsense with a timestamp I recognise instantly.

Which is exactly why the rule is **log it anyway**. You cannot tell, at the
time, which one you are in. The filter has to run later, in daylight, over a
record you did not curate as you made it. Curating at the moment of writing
means the daytime brain gets a veto over what the night brain is allowed to have
said, and the whole point was that the daytime brain has an interest.

So: THESIS · ANTITHESIS · ANOTHER BUMP · SYNTHESIS · CITE YOUR SOURCES · LOSE
THE ARGUMENT · LOG IT ANYWAY. It reads as a bit. Six of the seven are literal
instructions and I follow them.

## What I would tell somebody starting one of these

Timestamp everything, automatically, at a granularity you would find slightly
embarrassing. Never round it. Never batch your writing into a tidy session with
one date on it.

The timestamps are the cheapest metadata you will ever collect and they turned
out to be the most useful thing in the corpus — more useful than tags, more
useful than the link graph, which is saying something given how much of this
site is built on the link graph. They are the only field that records the
*conditions* a claim was made under, and a claim without its conditions is just
an opinion with a citation stapled to it.

It is 4am. It is always 4am here. Log it anyway.
