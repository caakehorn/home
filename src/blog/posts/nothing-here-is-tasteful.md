Somebody looked at this site and said it needed toning down. They meant it
kindly. They were describing, without noticing, about fifteen years of the web
converging on one page: a soft grey ground, a system font, a max-width of 65
characters, generous whitespace, one restrained accent colour, and a cookie
banner.

I want to be precise about the objection, because "I like it loud" is not an
argument and I am not making it.

## Restraint is not neutral

Every one of those defaults is a choice that somebody made, that worked, that
got copied until it stopped looking like a choice. That is what a convention
*is*. Fine. The problem is what happens next: once a look reads as neutral, it
starts to read as **credible**, and then anything outside it reads as
unserious — regardless of what it says.

That is a bad trade and we all took it. The most restrained, most tasteful,
most whitespace-generous pages on the web right now are the ones selling you
something. Taste got captured. It is the house style of the surveillance
economy, and a personal site that adopts it is wearing a uniform it was never
issued.

So the ground here is black or it is raw canvas. The type is a poster face. The
banner says GET FUCKED and EAT THE RICH and TRANS RIGHTS and it says them at
whatever size the marquee is running at, on every page, because they are not
decorations, they are the terms of service.

## Loud is not the same as careless

Here is the part I will defend hardest, because it is where the argument
usually collapses.

The contrast ratios are checked. The strobe on the lockout runs at about 1.5 Hz,
half the 3 Hz general-flash threshold in WCAG 2.3.1, and under
`prefers-reduced-motion` it stops flashing entirely and still takes the whole
screen for thirty seconds, because the *point* of it was never the flashing. The
map is reachable from the keyboard — arrows walk, Enter opens, Escape resets.
The canvas repaints only when something moves. Every animation duration on this
site derives from one variable that reduced-motion pins to a crawl.

None of that is in tension with the aesthetic. The idea that it would be is the
same category error as before: **accessibility got filed under "tasteful"** by
people who were mostly doing neither, and so the assumption is that a loud site
is an inaccessible one. A loud site is a site with high contrast and big type. If
you have to squint at it, that is a bug, and I want to hear about it.

## No cookies, no analytics, no taper

Three claims in the footer, all of them literal.

There is no analytics on this page. I do not know you are reading it. I will
never know how far down you got, whether you bounced, or which of these posts is
"performing". This is not a privacy position I am taking on your behalf — it is
selfish. The moment I can see a number go up, I will start writing for the
number, and everything above this line will quietly become a strategy.

Nothing here is tasteful. That is the specification, not an apology.
