The shell has a `rm` verb. Type `rm -rf /` at it and it refuses, and the refusal
is the only line in that whole terminal I wrote without hedging:

> the record does not delete. that is the entire point of the record.

That is not a joke about Unix. It is the operating rule of the entire building,
and I want to write down why, because I have broken it twice and both times it
cost me something I could not get back.

## What tidying actually does

Here is the thing nobody tells you about keeping notes on your own life for a
decade. The notes stop agreeing with each other. Not in the interesting way —
in the boring, embarrassing way where a page written in March says a thing
happened *because* of X, and a page written in November says the same thing
happened *in spite of* X, and both pages are written by someone completely
certain, and that someone is you.

The instinct is to fix it. Pick the better one. Merge them. Add a line at the
top saying *see also, superseded by*. I did this. It felt like maintenance.

It is not maintenance. It is the destruction of the only evidence you have that
you changed your mind, and — worse — of *what it felt like to believe the other
thing*. The March page is not a wrong version of the November page. It is a
record of a person who had a different set of facts and drew a defensible
conclusion from them. Delete it and you are left with a wiki that has always
agreed with you, which is not a record of a life. It is a press release.

## The rule, stated properly

**A contradiction between two pages is data about the gap between them.**

So: pages do not get merged, they get linked. Claims do not get corrected, they
get dated. When two pages disagree, the disagreement gets its own page and both
sides link to it. The site has 458 pages partly because I keep writing new ones
instead of fixing old ones, and I have come around to thinking that is the
correct failure mode to have.

There is a second-order effect I did not expect. Once you know nothing will be
tidied away, you write differently — you stop performing certainty, because the
performance is going into a permanent record next to the thing that will
contradict it in four months. You start writing *"as of tonight I think"*. Which
is what you meant the whole time.

## Where it does not apply

This is a rule about claims, not about cruelty. Things get removed from the wiki
for exactly one reason: they are about somebody else and they are not mine to
keep. That is not tidying, that is consent, and it is the one edit that never
needs a justification in the log.

Everything else stays. Including this post, when I inevitably come back in a
year and disagree with it.
